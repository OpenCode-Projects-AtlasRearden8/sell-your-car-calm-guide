# 03 · Safe Test Drives

**Goal of this chapter:** meet strangers, keep everyone safe, and lose nothing — not keys, not the car, not your cool.

## Screening before the drive (by phone/email, 5 min each)

Ask every serious inquirer:

1. **Name and phone number.** A real buyer gives both. Interrogate less, just note it.
2. **"When can you come, and who's coming with you?"** Daytime meetings are safest.
3. **"Will you be paying with cash or a cashier's check?"** Plants the payment expectation early (see [04 · Payment Safety](04-payment-safety.md)).

Red flags — cancel, don't negotiate:

- Won't give a phone number
- Asks you to bring the car *to them*
- Wants to pay via a link, gift card, or "escrow service" you've never heard of
- Story changes between messages

## Where and when to meet

**Best: a public place in daylight** — a shopping-center lot, a large coffee-shop lot, or (better) a **police-station parking lot** ("safe exchange zone"). Many departments offer them; check your local PD's website or just call.

**Do not** meet: at night, at your house (unless you have no choice — then meet outside, don't invite in), or somewhere the buyer picks.

## Before the keys move

- **Verify they have a license.** Photocopy/photograph their driver's license *before* the drive — or at least eyeball it and write down their name. It sounds awkward; say "I have to copy any test driver's license for insurance." True and no one argues.
- **Verify insurance or check your own.** Your own policy usually covers permitted drivers on a test drive, but *call your insurer* and confirm — see [05 · Notify DMV and Insurer](05-notify-dmv-and-insurer.md). State minimums and company rules vary.
- **Have a friend there if you can.** Not paranoia — logistics. Someone else holds the keys and watches.
- **Decide who drives first:** you. Do a 3-minute loop yourself (shows them the car works, warms it up, and gives you a feel for them), then hand over.
- **Set the route:** "Let's take a loop to the highway and back — about ten minutes." A fixed route they're told about beats a "where are you going" free-for-all.

## During the drive

- You ride along (always, unless you have a friend riding along and even then usually yes). You are still allowed to say "let's head back now."
- **Never let a stranger drive alone with your car.** Not to "show their mechanic," not "back in 20 minutes." No. If their mechanic really wants to look, meet the mechanic at a shop you also attend.
- Don't leave the title, registration papers, keys-to-anything-else, or your purse in the car. Registration card is fine to leave; the title never comes to a test drive.

## After the drive

- Walk the car with them — pre-own the flaws: "Bumper scratch, I priced it in." Honesty kills haggling ambushes.
- If they love it: good — get their full name and set a sale appointment. Do **not** take deposits by Zelle/Venmo from strangers who never saw the car ("deposit to hold" requests are a standard scam pattern).
- If they leave: re-lock, keys in pocket, move on to the next.

## Checklist — Safe Test Drives

**Before:** 
- [ ] Public daytime location chosen (police lot if nearby)
- [ ] Friend lined up (optional but great)
- [ ] Phone number and name collected
- [ ] Buyer's driver's license copied/photographed
- [ ] Insurance coverage for test drivers confirmed with my insurer
- [ ] Title, wallet, valuables NOT in the car
- [ ] Car warm, personal items out

**During:**
- [ ] I drive first / friend rides along
- [ ] Route told in advance
- [ ] Buyer never drives alone

**After:**
- [ ] Car re-locked, keys in pocket
- [ ] Flaws pointed out before the buyer found them
- [ ] No deposit taken outside the payment rules in [04](04-payment-safety.md)
