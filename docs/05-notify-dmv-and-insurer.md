# 05 · Notifying the DMV and Insurer

**Goal of this chapter:** the moment the sale is *actually* finished in the eyes of the law and your wallet. Skip this and you can get parking tickets, toll bills, or liability for accidents run by a stranger in your old car. Time: ~30 minutes, do it within 24 hours of the sale.

## Step 1 — Notify the DMV (10–15 min)

Every state differs slightly, but the pattern is:

1. **Find the right form.** Search **"[your state] DMV notice of sale"** or "report of sold vehicle." Names vary — *Notice of Transfer, Notice of Release of Liability, Vehicle Sold Notice.*
2. **Submit it on your state's timeline.** Some states (e.g., California, with its Release of Liability) want it within **5 days**; others within 10, 15, or 30. Many can be done **online in 5 minutes** — a few still want a mail-in postcard. Either way: do it.
3. **Keep proof.** Screenshot the online confirmation or keep the certified-mail receipt. If a ticket ever appears, this is your shield.
4. **Confirm the buyer's title got processed.** In some states you can check online in a week or two that the title no longer shows you. If the buyer never registered it, your notice of sale is the thing protecting you.

Some states also want: returned plates (e.g., NY), registration cancellation, or a final inspection on certain vehicles. Skim your DMV's "sold a car" page once — 5 minutes, worth it.

A ready-to-fill **Notice of Sale letter** (mail-in version) is in [07 · Templates](07-templates.md).

## Step 2 — Call your insurer (5–10 min)

- **Option A: Remove the car from your policy.** Simple, immediate, the right default.
- **Option B: Move the coverage onto your new car first**, then drop the old one — prevents a coverage gap if you're buying a replacement. Order matters: add new car, *then* drop old car, in the same call if you can.
- **Cancel anything attached to the old car:** re-bundled policies, roadside addon, or E-ZPass/plate transponder account.
- Ask about a **pro-rated refund** of any prepaid months — you shouldn't pay to insure a car sitting in a stranger's driveway.


## Step 3 — Loose ends (10 min)

- [ ] **Plates:** returned to the DMV (if required) or transferred to the new car (if allowed)
- [ ] **Registration refund/cancellation** filed if your state issues partial-year refunds
- [ ] **Toll transponder** unlinked or reassigned (this one generates surprise bills)
- [ ] **Cancel automatic payments** tied to the old car — insurance, maintenance plans, anything
- [ ] **Maintenance records kept.** No, you're done with the car — but keep the folder one more month in case of a dispute or tax question (see below).
- [ ] **Taxes:** most states tax the *buyer*, and your sale-to-purchase time window (e.g., 30–45 days in some states) can reduce sales tax on the new car. Worth a quick check before you buy your next one.

## Checklist — DMV & Insurer (within 24 hours of sale, ~30 min)

- [ ] Found and filed my state's notice-of-sale / release-of-liability form
- [ ] Confirmation screenshot or receipt saved in the sale folder
- [ ] Insurer: new car added (if any) and old car removed, refund confirmed
- [ ] Plates handled per state rules
- [ ] Toll transponder and subscriptions cancelled
- [ ] Sale folder (title copy, bill of sale, payment proof, DMV confirmation) kept for records

**That's it. The car is sold, the paperwork is done, and nobody can send you a bill for someone else's driving.** Next: [06 · Checklists](06-checklists.md).
