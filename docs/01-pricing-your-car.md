# 01 · Pricing Your Car

**Goal of this chapter:** end it knowing a fair ask price, a walk-away price, and why you chose them. Time: ~30 minutes plus a test-drive-free cleanup day later.

## Step 1 — Look up your car in three places (15 min)

Never price from one source — prices vary and outliers lie. Check three:

1. **Kelley Blue Book (kbb.com)** — instant-value tool; the number buyers will quote at you
2. **Edmunds (edmunds.com)** — " appraisal " / True Market Value; a good second opinion
3. **Your live local market** — search Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and (if relevant) eBay Motors sold listings for your exact model, year, trim, and mileage within ~100 miles. *Sold* prices matter more than asking prices; asking prices are hopes.

Write down all three numbers. If two are close and one is extreme, trust the two.

## Step 2 — Adjust for your car's real condition (10 min)

Use your honest condition, not your fondness for the car:

| Condition | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Excellent** | Looks and drives showroom-new; dealership-grade; rare |
| **Very good** | Minor wear, everything works, no mechanical issues (most well-kept cars) |
| **Good** | Normal wear, needs nothing now, small cosmetic issues |
| **Fair** | Needs mechanical or cosmetic work a buyer would have to pay for |

Be honest about the things you know: the check-engine light that comes and goes, the scraped bumper, worn tires. The buyer will find them — pricing them in now avoids renegotiation ambushes later.

## Step 3 — Set your three prices (5 min)

- **Ask price:** mid-market value ± a little room to negotiate (a common ballpark: market value + 5–10%, then round up to a clean number like $7,900, not $8,137)
- **Target price:** market value. This is what you actually expect to get.
- **Walk-away price:** the lowest you'll accept (often market − 10%). Write it down and *do not renegotiate it with yourself* on sale day with coffee and hope.

## Step 4 — Price against the competition (5 min)

Search your model one more time and ask: are you priced near the middle of the list? Bottom-third cars sell in days; top-third cars sit for weeks. If you need money slowly more than you need it fast, price high and wait. If you need it gone (moving, new car coming), price low and sell the first weekend.

## Step 5 — Prep the car for photos

Cheap high-return items: full wash and vacuum, remove personal items, clean windows, touch nothing you don't understand under the hood. Do **not** bother with paint correction or a dealer-detail ($200+) unless a $200 bump is realistic — for most cars a $15 wash is the right move.

**Gather your document pile while you're at it** — that's the next chapter: [02 · Title and Bill of Sale](02-title-and-bill-of-sale.md).

## Checklist — Pricing (one sitting, ~30 min)

- [ ] Looked up value on KBB, Edmunds, and live local listings
- [ ] Honest condition rating chosen (not the optimistic one)
- [ ] Ask price, target price, and walk-away price written down
- [ ] Compared my ask price against current local listings
- [ ] Car washed, vacuumed, personal items removed
- [ ] Gathered: title, current registration, maintenance records, spare keys
