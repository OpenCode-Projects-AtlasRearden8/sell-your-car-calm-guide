# 00 · Start Here

Selling a car privately gets you meaningfully more money than a trade-in — usually $1,000–$3,000 more on a typical used car. The catch is that it puts *you* in the dealer's chair: you price the car, meet strangers, hold the paperwork, and collect the money.

That is genuinely a lot. This guide breaks it into **five small stages**, each with a checklist sized for one sitting. You do not need to do it all in one day.

## How to use this guide

- **Read one chapter, do its checklist, stop.** Every chapter ends with a checklist you can finish in 15–60 minutes. That boundary is the point.
- **Print the checklists.** Open [`site/checklists.html`](../site/checklists.html) in a browser and press Ctrl+P, or use the master list in [06 · Checklists](06-checklists.md).
- **Templates are ready to copy.** A plain-English bill of sale and a notice-of-sale letter are in [07 · Templates](07-templates.md).

## The three rules that keep this calm

1. **Slow is safe.** Any buyer pressuring you to meet *right now*, pay *right now*, or skip paperwork is telling you something useful. Real buyers wait.
2. **Paperwork before people.** Confirm you can actually transfer the title *before* you list the car, not after someone is standing in your driveway.
3. **The transfer is not done until the DMV has it.** Until the sale is reported to your state DMV, you are still legally on the hook for the car — tickets, tolls, even accidents. Chapter 5 is not optional.

## A realistic timeline

- **Week 1:** Price the car, gather paperwork, gather your documents (Stage 1–2)
- **Week 1–3:** List it, screen inquiries, run test drives (Stage 3)
- **Sale day:** Verify payment, sign everything in one sitting, hand over keys (Stage 4)
- **Sale day + 1:** Notify the DMV and insurer (Stage 5)

## What this guide is not

This is general educational information, not legal or financial advice. Title rules, emissions requirements, and sale-notice deadlines **differ by state**. When this guide says "check your state's rules," it means your state DMV's website — usually a 5-minute lookup. For odd situations ( liens, inherited cars, out-of-state buyers ), talk to your DMV or an attorney.
